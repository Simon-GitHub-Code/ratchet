# Only product questions reach the owner; every other choice is an assumption the hand-over states

Ratchet is for hobbyists and solo founders who are not engineers. The owner's principle is that Ratchet helps a person's creative flow and never blocks it. He wants to be asked about the product he is building and about consequential direction, and about nothing else. The dogfood run of 2026-11-13 took a fresh project through `/ratchet:init`, the tracker setup, `/ratchet:adopt`, a spec and its tickets, and asked 19 questions on the way. Eight were about the product: its purpose, its user, what makes it better, where it is used, the first usable part, what waits, what becomes of a read item, what a duplicate link is. Eleven had an answer a default gives: a table of defaults to accept, the stack, whether it has a screen, the application types, the life events of a new project, the tools, three of the tracker setup's, the test seams, and whether to publish a ticket split. ADR 0022 had already ruled out a confirmation; the rule was prose, and each skill's own text kept its questions.

We decided eight things.

1. **A question is a product question or it is not asked.** Ask the owner when the choice decides what becomes of something of the user's (kept, deleted, merged, duplicated, overwritten, shared), who may see or do it, or what the user can do at all, and nothing the owner said or answered settles it. Ask all of a step's open questions in the user's words, in one message, each with your recommended answer first and why, so the owner can accept them all at once. A choice an agent wrote down earlier (a spec, a conjecture, an **Assumed** line) does not settle it, and where the owner's words do settle it the spec follows them. Assume, and list under **Assumed**, anything a later change reverses without loss to the user: order, wording, layout, timing, limits, tools, and how it is built. The same words are in the `AGENTS.md` template, the method's stance, `records.md` and the fork test of `pipeline.md`. A first wording ("what this should do, for whom, what matters") covered every behaviour, so it could not separate ask from assume: in a rerun the spec step decided what a pasted duplicate becomes, against what the owner had wanted, and the conjecture called the choice settled because the spec had written it down. ADR 0022's `direction` and `irreversible` are both product questions. Its third kind, `tooling`, goes: the tools question of `/ratchet:adopt` and of `/ratchet:safeguard` is an assumption now. This supersedes ADR 0017's "the owner is asked one question about tools" and ADR 0022's decision 2 where it names the tools question. A step's product questions go in one message, in the words of someone using the product, with no method term; the conjecture's questions join the spec step's message when they are known at that point, and otherwise form one message of their own. An earlier wording put each question alone, one at a time, while the spec step already put all of its questions in one dialog: an owner who accepts every recommendation now does so once per step.

2. **What replaces each removed question.** The agent takes the default the evidence supports, acts, and ends its reply with an **Assumed** block: one plain line per assumption, what it chose and why in a clause, what changing it later costs (what changes, roughly how much work, and whether anything built is redone: a tool bound at adoption costs a change of its own that swaps the package in the dependency manifest and its lockfile, rewrites the tool's config, the gate's recipe, its planted fault and their snapshots, needs the owner's own edit of the guard manifest and `just settings`, and is merged by him; a stack changed after adoption has no step yet that binds the gates again), and what the owner can say to change it. That block is the one place the owner reads them, at the end of the step he just ran; the decision itself lands in the record a later agent reads (`AGENTS.md`, `PRODUCT.md`, the binding), so an assumption he overturns later in his own words is overturned where it is kept. Init's defaults table, its stack and its interface; adopt's application types, its life events on an empty repository, and its tools; safeguard's tools: each is an `[[assume]]` entry of `skills/method/questions.toml`. Overturning one is a procedure in `records.md`, *Overturning an assumption*: the owner's words are kept where the next conjecture reads them, the record that holds the choice changes by the route for its kind (for a life event, `/ratchet:safeguard <event>`, never a hand edit of the binding), and what was built on the old choice is flagged.

3. **The one outward act keeps its question, in plain words.** Init creates a private remote only from an answer to "Should this project be kept on this computer only, or also as a private copy on your GitHub account?", asked only when a forge login exists and no remote does. A wrong guess publishes the owner's work to an account; that is a direction with consequences, and the old defaults table hid it in a confirmation.

4. **The registry and a test make the rule structural.** `skills/method/questions.toml` lists each question a Ratchet skill asks (its key, the skills that ask it, its kind and its words) and each question a default now answers. A skill asks with `**Ask** `<key>`: "<the words>"` and states a default with `**Assume** `<key>``. `hooks/tests/test_owner_questions.py` is red on an unregistered key, a kind other than `product`, words that differ from the registry's, a question no skill asks, an assumption no skill states, a method term in a product question, and a sentence in any skill page that tells an agent to ask, confirm, approve, let the owner pick, get their go-ahead or wait for an answer, or that puts a quoted question to them, without a registered key, and a sentence that puts questions to the owner one at a time, alone or one per message; "until you ask" asks even after a negation. `/ratchet:init interactive` and `/ratchet:adopt interactive`, which turned every default into a question, are deleted: the owner overturns a default in words.

5. **A conjecture accounts for each thing the owner said, and asks each fork left open.** `/ratchet:conjecture` adds a *What you asked for* table to `## Conjecture`: one row per thing the owner said that the ticket touches, quoted from where the record keeps it, marked **built** (with the criterion or story that holds it), **assumed** or **left out** with the reason. Each behaviour the spec chose that nothing he said settles goes through the fork test. One the test gives him is `(left open)` until asked, every one, and is never marked **assumed** or **added**: once answered, the row quotes his answer and is **built**, so the written table holds no `(left open)` row; the step never says no question was needed while such a row is open. One the test gives the agent is written `(not asked)` and marked **assumed**. A spec criterion that contradicts his words is corrected and the row says so. An addition that changes what he sees, keeps or can do goes through the rule; only an addition he never sees (a title's markup shown as plain text) is listed as **added**, written `(not asked)`. In three of three sampled runs the table had written `(left open)` with **added**, and one of those rows made a product choice without him; the skill's example table now shows each kind of row, and a test holds it to the rule. Adoption writes his answers word for word under `## In the owner's words` in the design brief, so the table has a source. Adopt also asks its questions at step 3, in the first minutes, rather than at steps 6 and 11, so the owner answers and leaves.

6. **A repository with code is always asked whether others reach it.** ADR 0028 holds that a machine cannot see a life event reliably, so the owner declares it; the route's sign patterns miss a deploy job in a workflow of another name and a production address in an example settings file. So on a repository that already has code, adopt asks `event.deployed` every time, recommending "no" only when the tree shows no sign, and asks about irreplaceable data and people relying on it when the tree shows a sign or the owner said yes. Only an empty repository assumes none.

7. **Preferences come first, in words.** With the `interactive` modes gone, an owner who already knows what he wants types it after the command (`/ratchet:init no GitHub`, `/ratchet:adopt use ESLint`). A preference chooses among the options a row allows: within those it wins over the default, settles any question it answers but a life event's, and is listed under **Assumed** as "you asked for". It never turns a floor row to `none`, lowers a threshold, skips a safeguard, or declares or denies a life event. One that asks for that is answered in plain words with the route that exists (a dispute the owner decides and a superseding decision record for loosening; the life-event question, or `/ratchet:safeguard <event>` after adoption), and is listed as not applied. In a verifier's run the agent refused such a preference by its own judgment; the rule is now written once, in `records.md` under *A preference typed after a command*, and both skills point at it.

8. **The registry has no reviewer of its own.** A change to `questions.toml` is read only as part of its pull request, and the merge judge treats it like any skill page. Sending registry changes to the owner is not built.

The tracker setup, `to-spec` and `to-tickets` are mattpocock-skills', and ADR 0009's floor stands: the owner types them. Their questions are governed only by the `## Asking the owner` rule in the `AGENTS.md` template, which every agent in the repository reads, and which now says that a tracker or labels already recorded, where tests reach the code, a ticket split and a draft to confirm are the agent's to choose and state. Decided 2026-11-13.

Supersedes, in part: ADR 0017's question about tools, and ADR 0022's `tooling` kind and its logging of init's and adopt's answers. The rest of both stands.

## Refutation criterion

This is wrong in either direction. Too tight: in the first ten first-hour runs after it, the owner overturns more than one assumption in five from the **Assumed** blocks, or says of a removed question that he wanted it asked. Too loose: a run asks the owner a question he says a default would have answered. The first-hour harness counts questions by class; a run that asks one outside the rule, or any confirmation, refutes the claim that the rule holds in practice and not only on the page. Too loose the other way: a run's spec or conjecture decides a choice the rule gives the owner, or contradicts what he said, and no question was asked.

Known residue. The test reads words, not meaning: a question phrased outside its vocabulary ("see what the owner thinks") passes, as does a negation other than "until" before the asking words, and a non-product question someone registers as `product` with matching skill text, which nothing reviews (decision 8). The Spec role's copy of the fork test in `workflows/pipeline.js` predates decision 1 and still sends a difference only in what the user sees to the owner: it asks more than the rule, never less. Questions a companion plugin's skill asks are outside the registry. ADR 0022's log of answers now records only the feature pipeline's per-ticket questions, which never repeat under one key, so its `also:` proposal cannot form; ADR 0022's own criterion removes `questions.py` and the `also:` line, in a change to the route.

## Considered options

- Keep the rule as prose in each skill: rejected, that is ADR 0022, and the run asked eleven non-product questions under it.
- Show the assumptions in a file the owner can open: rejected, a file he never opens is not a place he reads. The end of the reply is.
- Keep `interactive` modes for an owner who wants every choice: rejected, they ask non-product questions by design. A preference typed after the command (decision 7) sets a choice before it is made, and a sentence in the owner's words overturns any default after.
- Cap the conjecture at one question: rejected, a spec can leave several forks open, and a cap made the rest silent assumptions.
- Ask nothing at init on an empty directory and infer the product from the first spec: rejected, the purpose, the user and where it is used are product questions no experiment settles, and every later default leans on them.
